Research

I develop rigorous, practice-grounded theory for foundation models across training, inference, and deployment. My work studies how data, optimization, and architecture determine what models learn, how they use context, and how their capabilities shape system-level outcomes.

Central question

Which properties of modern learning systems are essential for predicting how foundation models learn, behave, and shape system-level outcomes?

Past and Current Research

Across the three stages, I build tractable theoretical models that retain the data distributions, architectural structures, and strategic interactions that materially affect modern learning systems.

01
Training

Data Distribution and Architecture Shape Optimization Efficacy

How do training dynamics select the learned function?

My work shows that an optimizer cannot be understood in isolation: its effectiveness depends on how its geometry interacts with data heterogeneity and architectural structure. I use the performance gap between Muon and Adam as a concrete test case for this broader principle.

Heavy-tailed data and associative memory

Muon’s advantage is concentrated in associative-memory components, where it learns low-frequency associations more effectively under imbalanced data.

Curvature and optimizer geometry

Gradient–Hessian alignment lets Muon incur a smaller second-order curvature penalty than Adam, with heavier-tailed data amplifying the advantage.

Optimizer-dependent representations

Under feature imbalance, different optimizers learn distinct representation subspaces with systematic differences in robustness and transferability.

02
Inference

Context Dynamics and Architecture Shape Information Utilization

How do learned functions use context and history?

At inference, the key unit of analysis shifts from individual tokens to dependencies across a sequence. I study how the statistical structure of context—including nonstationarity—interacts with architectural inductive bias to determine what information a model can use.

Bayesian computation in context

My analyses characterize In-Context Learning as Bayesian model averaging over latent tasks and extend that mechanism to multiple Chain-of-Thought reasoning trajectories.

Long-horizon visual generation

For autoregressive image and video models, I connect inefficiency and degradation to sparse visual signals, finite-context history forgetting, and accumulated local prediction errors.

Selected work
  • What and How Does In-Context Learning Learn? AISTATS 2025
  • Unveiling the Statistical Foundations of Chain-of-Thought Prompting Methods JMLR 2026
  • Error Analyses of Auto-Regressive Video Diffusion Models JMLR 2026
03
Deployment

Model Performance and Computational Cost Shape System Outcomes

How do inference performance and cost shape strategic behavior?

Once models are deployed, heterogeneous users trade off the performance and cost of adaptation methods. Their choices create congestion in shared infrastructure, linking statistical performance to user welfare, platform profit, and equilibrium outcomes.

Heterogeneous population interactions

Graphon mean-field games model structured populations and yield equilibrium guarantees, estimation methods, and learning algorithms when the interaction graph is unknown.

User–platform equilibria

Quantitative models of ICL and supervised fine-tuning reveal how improvements in statistical performance or compute cost can change congestion non-monotonically.

Future Research Directions

My long-term goal is an end-to-end predictive theory of how interventions in data, optimization, and architecture alter inference-time capabilities and, ultimately, system-level outcomes.

Thrust 1 · Training

Toward a Unified Theory of Foundation-Model Training

What principles persist across optimizers and architectures?

Relative Generalization Invariance—approximately stable differences in token-level validation loss after an initial transient—offers an empirical starting point for identifying shared structure in generalization dynamics.

Near term
Test invariances across scale, pretraining, post-training, optimizers, architectures, and representation learning.
Long term
Separate shared, data-dependent dynamics from optimizer- and architecture-specific deviations to guide better designs.
Thrust 2 · Training → Inference

Connecting Training Dynamics to Inference-Time Capabilities

How can training dynamics predict the capabilities expressed at inference?

My initial work links gradient-descent training to the emergence of attention patterns that use positional embeddings to aggregate the contextual information needed for prediction.

Near term
Track how induction, decomposition, verification, and self-correction emerge under pretraining and reinforcement learning.
Long term
Map data, objectives, optimizers, and architectures to capability emergence, reliability, and inference-time scaling.
Thrust 3 · Inference → Deployment

Closing the Loop between Capabilities and User Behavior

How do inference capabilities and user behavior co-evolve?

Building on models of personalization choices and prompt-adaptive decoding, I will study the two-way feedback among model capability, user behavior, data, resource demand, and platform incentives.

Near term
Develop dynamic models of user decisions and model updates, including congestion, biased feedback, and performance disparities.
Long term
Identify which statistical and computational properties create economic value and improve human welfare.

Together, these directions connect what a model learns, what it can do with context, and how those capabilities reshape the systems in which it is deployed.
